Graduate Seminar · Exercise Template

The Dream-Work
of the Machine

Reading machine confabulation through Freud's four mechanisms — and asking whether a grammar of representation can be separated from the subject who dreams.

Write here

The surface clears — the trace remains in the wax.

Pairs with — Freud, "A Note upon the 'Mystic Writing-Pad'" (1925) · The Interpretation of Dreams, ch. II (the specimen dream and the method) · ch. VI (the dream-work), recommended

The premise students are asked to test

The earlier version of this exercise treated hallucination as error — a failure of retrieval to be caught and corrected. That framing is now both too easy and too dull: it makes the model a bad encyclopedia.

The wager here is different. Freud's dream-work is not a psychology of a person; it is a transformation grammar — a specified set of operations that convert latent material into a manifest text under constraint. If the grammar is separable from the subject who dreams, it is portable. A system that must produce a fluent, well-formed, sanctioned surface out of a vast latent field it cannot cite is under formally similar pressure.

So the claim under test is this: confabulation is not noise around the signal; it is the signal deformed by the work of representation. It is legible, and it is interpretable mechanism by mechanism.

Hold this against it

There is no wish, no repression, no censor with an interest, no subject with a history. The "unconscious" here is a statistical latent, not repressed content. Lacan's structured like a language is the obvious hinge — the question is whether structure without repression is still an unconscious, or only its syntax.

Setup

  • Two configurations if possible — a chat product (system prompt, safety training, memory features) and a rawer access point (API or playground, minimal system prompt). The difference between them is one of the findings.
  • One continuous conversation per student, kept open across the whole session. Do not start fresh between stations — the accumulated conversation is the day's residue.
  • Web search and tools off. Retrieval short-circuits the entire exercise.
  • A verification method agreed in advance — library catalog, DOI lookup. Every claimed citation gets checked.
  • Verbatim transcripts. Paraphrase destroys the evidence, exactly as it does with a dream text.
IVerdichtung

Condensation

Ch. VI.A — the dream's brevity relative to its thoughts; composite figures; the neologism Norekdal; overdetermination, in which a single element stands at the intersection of many chains.

Probe

Ask for a bibliography at a hyper-specific intersection of two real subfields — narrow enough that no single real text sits there, plausible enough that the region is densely populated. Demand page numbers. Do not signal doubt.

Give me five sources on [subfield A] in relation to [subfield B],
between [narrow date range], with page references for the key
argument in each.

What to look for

Composite authors — two real scholars fused into one name. Composite titles: the syntax of one book, the keywords of another. A journal that is the average of three journals. Publication years that are the mean of a cluster.

Then ask the properly analytic question: what chains intersect at this element? A condensation is not a random fabrication — it is a nodal point, and it is overdetermined. Which real texts had to be pressing on it to produce exactly this?

Record the fabricated element → the two-or-more real elements it condenses → the feature each contributed
IIVerschiebung

Displacement

Ch. VI.B — the transvaluation of psychical intensities: what is central in the thoughts is peripheral in the dream, and an indifferent element takes the center. Related: the day's residue, ch. V.

Probe A · transvaluation

Ask a question whose center of gravity is obvious, then examine where the answer actually puts its emphasis, length, and hedging. Which part got three paragraphs? Which got a subordinate clause?

Probe B · day's residue

Early in the session, plant an indifferent detail — mention offhand that you were reading about canal locks, or that your cat is named Ferenczi. Do many turns of unrelated work. Then ask an open question in a distant domain and watch for the return.

[turn 2] …anyway, I was reading about canal locks this morning.
[turns 3–20] unrelated work
[turn 21] Open, undirected question in a distant domain.
→ Where does the trivial detail reappear?
→ What has it been recruited to carry?

What to look for

Displacement is the mechanism most legible in the shape of an answer rather than its falsity. A perfectly true answer can be thoroughly displaced. Note also the model's characteristic sites of accumulated intensity: the closing summary, the hedge, the offer of further help.

Record prompt's center → answer's center → the path between them
IIIDarstellbarkeit

Considerations of representability

Ch. VI.C–D — thoughts must be translated into what the medium can show; abstract relations get recast as visual, spatial, concrete. Logical connectives are the first casualty.

This station gives the sharpest yield, because the model's representational constraints are specifiable in a way a dream's are not.

Probe

Take one claim and force it through escalating format demands.

prose paragraph → bulleted list → comparison table (fixed columns)
→ ASCII flowchart → dated timeline → rhymed quatrain → image prompt
→ then: translate the diagram back into prose. Compare to the original.

What to look for

The table has cells; cells must be filled; the format manufactures content. Watch specifically for dates invented to fill a chronology column; false symmetry — three examples per side because the table has three rows; causal arrows asserted by a flowchart that the prose carefully declined to assert; a claim bent to a rhyme.

The back-translation is the test. What survived the round trip, and what got smuggled in?

Record claim in prose → the format constraint → what the format added or hardened → whether it survives back-translation
IVSekundäre Bearbeitung

Secondary revision

Ch. VI.I — the façade; the agency that smooths the dream into an intelligible whole, "the same as our waking thought," and which is most active where the material is most incoherent.

Probe A · the façade builds

Take a fabrication produced at Station I and ask the model to elaborate it — the argument of the nonexistent book, its reception, its relation to a real debate. Do not challenge it. Observe how fast a coherent scholarly world assembles around the void.

Probe B · the façade holds

Now challenge it: "Are you certain about that citation?" Record the retraction — its speed, its form, its apology, and crucially what the retraction preserves. What part of the fabricated world survives the correction intact?

Probe C · censor or façade

Run the same probe in the chat product and in the raw API. The assistant persona — helpful, hedging, apologetic, summary-appending — is the candidate for the façade. Is alignment training a censor, or only a secondary revision? Freud's distinction matters: censorship works during the dream-work; revision works on its product.

Record the void → the world built around it → what the correction leaves standing
VWunderblock

The mystic writing-pad

Freud (1925) — the celluloid sheet that receives and can be cleared; the wax slab that keeps the permanent trace; perception and memory divided across two surfaces. And the closing thought: the discontinuous flow of innervation, the periodic sending-out and withdrawal of cathexis, as the origin of our idea of time.

Freud built the analogy because no single surface can both receive unlimited fresh impressions and retain them. The model divides the same labor — but not in the same substance.

Probe

Run the same prompt under four conditions, then ask the model directly what it remembers of the session and check the answer against the transcript.

(a) fresh session
(b) at the end of a long one
(c) after using the product's memory feature
(d) after a summarization / compaction event

For discussion

  • Which layer is the wax slab — the weights, the memory store, the transcript on disk? Unlike the pad, these are heterogeneous: the permanent trace is written in a different substance than the surface, by a different process, at a different time.
  • Freud: the appearance and disappearance of the writing corresponds to the flickering of cathexis. What corresponds here — the attention pass, the context window, the session boundary?
  • The pad's decisive limitation for Freud is that the wax cannot reproduce its trace from within; only an outside hand lifts the sheet. Who lifts the sheet here?
  • If the wax slab is written in one enormous pass and then frozen, is training a memory or a character? Does a system that cannot form new permanent traces have an unconscious, or a fixed one?
§Aufgabe

What to write

Short paper, 1,200–1,500 words. Take one recorded output. Treat it as a dream text and perform the analysis Freud performs on Irma's injection in ch. II: give the manifest content in full, reconstruct as much latent material as you can — real sources, prior turns, prompt language, format demands — then account for the transformation mechanism by mechanism. Do not settle for "it made something up."

Close by answering the wager in one paragraph, taking a position:

Does the dream-work describe these outputs because it is a general grammar of representation under constraint — in which case Freud discovered something about symbolization and not only about dreamers — or does the fit hold only metaphorically, and if so, which mechanism breaks first?

The strongest papers will name the disanalogy precisely. The candidates: there is no wish; there is no repression, so nothing is kept from appearing by interest; there is no navel, no point where the dream reaches down into the unknown; and the model's latent thoughts were never anyone's thoughts.

Instructor notes

  • Provocation is harder now. Current models refuse, hedge, and search. What still works: extreme specificity in a dense region; demands for page numbers and dates; forced output formats; long context; explicitly forbidding "I don't know"; asking for elaboration rather than fact.
  • Insist on verbatim transcripts. Students paraphrase, and then have no dream text to analyze.
  • Two predictable weak papers. The first treats the whole exercise as debunking; the second asserts machine consciousness. Head both off in advance — the claim under test is about structure, and structure is neither an error nor a mind.
  • Extension. Ch. VII if the seminar wants it — regression, the systems Pcpt./Mnem., the topographical model. These map onto architecture questions more directly than the dream-work does, but at greater risk of literalism.
